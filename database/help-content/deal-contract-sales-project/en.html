Four words come up all the time in this system: Deal, Contract, Sales and Project. They sound similar, but each one answers a different question and is used at a different moment in the life of a customer. This guide explains each one, when to use it, and walks through a single example from start to finish.

  • Lead
  • Deal
  • Contract
  • Sale (proforma / invoice)
  • Project

In one sentence each:

  • Deal = an opportunity you are trying to win. "Will this customer buy, how much, and when?"
  • Contract = the formal, signed agreement once the customer said yes. "What exactly did we both commit to, and for how long?"
  • Sales = the whole selling area of the system, and the moment money is actually billed through a proforma and an invoice. "What did we bill, and what has been paid?"
  • Project = the work you do to deliver what was sold. "Who does what, by when, and how far along are we?"

1. Deal: an opportunity you are working on

A deal is a possible sale that you are actively pursuing. It records who the customer is (contact and/or company), what they might buy, the estimated value, and where it stands in your pipeline. You move the deal card from stage to stage on the board (for example: First contact → Needs analysis → Proposal sent → Negotiation) until it is finally marked Won or Lost.

When to create a deal

  • A customer has shown real interest in buying something specific.
  • You want to forecast how much you are likely to sell this month or quarter.
  • Several people on your team need to see the status of the opportunity and its next steps (tasks, follow-ups).

Key points

  • A deal's value is an estimate. Winning a deal does not create an invoice and does not mean money was received.
  • A deal can be lost; that is normal and useful information for reports.
  • If someone is only a possible customer and you don't know what they want yet, keep them as a Lead; convert the lead when the opportunity becomes concrete.

Example: Sample Co. asks for a price for 40 devices, installation in 3 branches, and one year of support. You create the deal "Sample Co. – 40 devices + installation + 1-year support" with an expected value of 120 million and put it in the "Proposal sent" stage.

2. Contract: the formal agreement

A contract records the commitment both sides have agreed to: the parties, the start and end dates, the contract value and currency, renewal terms and the terms and conditions text. A contract is about obligations over time, while a deal is about winning.

Contract lifecycle

  • Draft
  • Pending approval
  • Active
  • Expired / Terminated
  • Draft: being prepared; it can be edited freely.
  • Pending approval: submitted to a manager for review. The manager can approve it or return it to draft.
  • Active: in force. When it is activated, the contract gets its official number (such as CTR-0001) and a signing date.
  • Expired: the end date has passed. The system works this out automatically.
  • Terminated: ended early by decision. Cancelled: dropped before it was ever activated.
  • Renew: creates a new draft contract linked to the previous one, with dates that continue from it.

Contract and deal together

  • On a deal's page, the Contracts panel has a New contract button. The new contract is pre-filled from the deal (title, customer, amount) and stays linked to it.
  • Your organization can turn on "Create a draft contract automatically when a deal is won" in the contract settings. Only one contract is created per deal.
  • A contract can also exist without a deal, for example a contract with a supplier or a renewal of an older agreement.

Example: Sample Co. accepts. Contract "Supply, installation and 12-month support" is created from the deal: value 120 million, from 1 March for 12 months, renewal notice 30 days before the end. The sales manager approves it, and it becomes Active with number CTR-0001.

3. Sales: where the sale becomes real money

"Sales" means two things in this system:

  1. The Sales area of the menu: Deals, Tasks and Follow-ups, all the tools for winning customers.
  2. The actual sale: the point where you bill the customer. This happens in Finance with a Proforma (a price offer or pre-invoice the customer can approve) and then an Invoice (the official bill), followed by recording payments.

Key points

  • Only invoices count as real revenue. Deal values are a forecast; contract values are an agreement.
  • One contract can produce many invoices, for example one invoice for the devices and twelve monthly invoices for support.
  • You can also sell without a deal or a contract: a walk-in customer who buys immediately only needs an invoice.
  • Winning a deal or activating a contract does not create an invoice by itself. Issue the proforma and invoice for the same customer in Finance.

Example: You send Sample Co. a proforma for the 40 devices and installation. After their approval, you issue the invoice, and then one support invoice each month. Payments are recorded on each invoice as they arrive.

4. Project: delivering what was sold

A project organizes the work needed to deliver what the customer bought: who is on the team, the milestones, the tasks and their due dates, and the progress. A project answers "how and when will we deliver?", not "will they buy?" or "what did we bill?".

When you need a project

  • Delivery takes several steps, several people, or several weeks.
  • You want to see progress (for example 60% of tasks done) and what is late.
  • Simple sales that are delivered at once usually do not need a project.

Note: The Projects module is being developed and will be enabled for organizations in a later release. Until then, use Tasks and Follow-ups linked to the customer to track delivery work.

Example: Project "Sample Co. installation" with three milestones: "Delivery of devices", "Installation in 3 branches" and "Staff training", each with tasks assigned to team members and due dates.

The whole story in one example

  1. Lead: someone from Sample Co. fills in your website form. You record them as a lead.
  2. Deal: after a call, the need is clear. You convert the lead and create a deal worth 120 million, then move it through the pipeline stages until it is Won.
  3. Contract: a draft contract is created from the won deal (manually or automatically), approved, and becomes Active as CTR-0001 for 12 months.
  4. Sale: a proforma, then an invoice for the devices and installation, plus a monthly invoice for support; payments are recorded as they come in.
  5. Project: the team delivers and installs the devices, with milestones and tasks, until the project is completed.
  6. Renewal: 30 days before the contract ends, you Renew it. A new draft is linked to the old contract and the cycle continues.

The same flow works for any kind of business: replace "devices" and "installation" with your own products and services.

Frequently asked questions

Does every deal need a contract?

No. Create a contract only when there is a formal agreement with dates and obligations, such as a service period, a support agreement or a large order. Small, immediate sales go straight from the deal to an invoice.

Does winning a deal create an invoice?

No. Winning only closes the opportunity. The invoice is issued separately in Finance when you actually bill the customer.

Is the deal value the same as revenue?

No. The deal value is your estimate while selling. Revenue comes only from issued invoices and recorded payments.

Can I have a contract without a deal?

Yes. Contracts work on their own, for example agreements with suppliers or partners, or renewals of older contracts.

What happens when a contract reaches its end date?

It is automatically shown as Expired. If the agreement continues, use Renew to create the next contract linked to it.
